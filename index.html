Alexandria's CXO priorities.

The top-down frame coming out of Q1 2026: a life-science REIT working through a lab-space downturn, concentrating on its Megacampus platform, recycling out of non-core, and pacing development to demand. Everything below ladders up to these three.

01 · Megacampus concentration Concentrate on the Megacampus platform and recycle out of non-core.

The Megacampus platform generated 78% of annual rental revenue as of March 31, 2026. Alexandria is reducing non-core holdings from 12% at year-end 2025 toward 5–10% by year-end 2026 — $2.9B of dispositions planned, $2.18B already identified.

02 · Deleverage Fund deleveraging through the $2.9B disposition program.

Net debt to EBITDA was 6.8x in Q1 2026, with plans to bring it down in the second half against $4.2B of liquidity. The disposition and partial-interest program is the central path — so transaction timing is the swing factor.

03 · Pipeline pacing Pace the development pipeline to demand and re-lease through the downturn.

1.9M sf is under construction at 77% leased; another 1.6M sf across five projects is under strategic review through 2027 — continue, reposition, or sell. Year-end 2026 occupancy guidance was lowered to about 87%.

Where could reliable, queryable data take cost and time out of the work?

Updated after the June 8 coffee — six places where manual work and scattered data slow Alexandria down today, ordered by likelihood to land. Open each one, tell us where you're warm or cold, and rank what rings true.

— Reporting & ObservabilityRevenue recognition lags the work that’s already done. Rank

Alexandria can’t recognize GAAP revenue until a tenant’s improvements are delivered and they move in. One building with a major professional-services tenant is running $2–3M a month in unrecognized revenue, with daily pressure to report delivery dates, burn, and cash flow. Accounting and development feel it at every quarter-end and investor report.

The opportunityTrack TI delivery and burn against leases so revenue is recognized sooner.What it looks likeA live TI-delivery tracker that reads each lease's improvement scope and dollar allowance, watches project burn and milestones against it, and flags the exact date revenue can be recognized. Accounting gets a same-day, building-by-building view of unrecognized revenue — and a feed that drops clean numbers straight into the quarter-end close and investor reporting.
— Extraction & AbstractionEvery region models cash flow differently. Rank

San Diego and Seattle build cash-flow projections in their own formats, and accounting re-standardizes all of it. Development leaders re-key numbers by hand to feed accounting and reporting. A unified model at ingestion would let each region plug numbers in and hand clean data downstream.

The opportunityStandardize regional financial and cash-flow models into one queryable schema at ingestion.What it looks likeAn ingestion engine that takes San Diego's and Seattle's models in whatever format they arrive and maps every line to one canonical schema, with click-through to the original source cell. Each region keeps working the way it likes; accounting stops re-keying and queries a single consolidated, audit-traceable model across the portfolio.
— Extraction & AbstractionLease obligations live in people’s heads and PDF leases. Rank

Milestones, key dates, and what is and isn’t owed are tracked by hand; when a tenant asks where something sits, the answer means re-reading the lease. Teams also over-deliver on items not owed, which leaks cost. Asset services, legal, and development all feel it.

The opportunitySource-linked lease abstraction with natural-language lookup on terms, milestones, and obligations.What it looks likeEvery lease and amendment abstracted into structured terms — options, escalations, recoveries, key dates — each field linked back to the exact clause. Anyone can ask “what do we owe on Suite 400, and by when?” and get a sourced answer in seconds, with option deadlines and recovery exceptions pushed to the asset manager before they're missed.
— Automation & AgentsLegal is a five-person choke point. Rank

Contracts, lease terms, and change orders all funnel through a five-person legal team with a real backlog — a routine change order can wait behind nine others. Development and procurement lose days waiting on processing.

The opportunityAccelerate change-order and contract processing with policy-checked, system-ready outputs.What it looks likeA review queue where each change order and contract is extracted, checked against Alexandria's policy and approval thresholds, and returned as a system-ready summary with the risk items surfaced. The five-person team reviews and approves instead of reading line by line — clearing the backlog so a routine change order moves in hours, not behind nine others.
— Automation & AgentsThe largest opex line runs without its own data. Rank

Asset services is the biggest team and the biggest operating expense, on call 24/7. Tenant-usage data (HVAC, facilities) that could extend equipment life and cut energy isn’t yet used, and receivables tracking is a constant focus. Asset services and finance feel it.

The opportunityTurn tenant-usage data into equipment and energy savings, and speed receivables, in the largest opex line.What it looks likeA console that pulls HVAC and facilities telemetry alongside lease and billing data to flag equipment trending toward failure, energy that can be dialed back off-hours, and receivables aging past terms. Asset services shifts from 24/7 reactive to prioritized and predictive — extending equipment life and protecting the biggest operating-expense line.
— Reporting & ObservabilityLeadership lacks fast, self-serve answers on the portfolio. Rank

Occupancy, NOI, renewals, and tenant health sit across multiple systems, and a data-driven president needs quick reads to pace decisions. Pulling them together is manual.

The opportunityA conversational portfolio view answering natural-language questions on occupancy, NOI, and renewals.What it looks likeOne conversational surface over occupancy, NOI, renewals, and tenant health, unified across systems. The president asks in plain language — “show me Seattle assets under 90% leased with renewals inside 12 months” — and gets a structured answer instantly, ready for the next capital or pacing decision.
